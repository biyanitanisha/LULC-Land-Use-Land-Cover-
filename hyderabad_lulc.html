Method

This map classifies land use and land cover across the Hyderabad metropolitan region using a Sentinel-2 surface reflectance composite averaged over January – June 2025, built and processed entirely in Google Earth Engine[cite: 1]. A CART decision-tree classifier (ee.Classifier.smileCart) was trained on hand-digitised reference points spanning four classes — waterbody, built-up, green spaces, and barren land — using the red, green, and blue reflectance bands (B4, B3, B2) sampled at 10 m resolution[cite: 1]. The trained model was then applied to classify every pixel within the region of interest, and its output was checked against an independent set of validation points using a standard error matrix, producer's accuracy, and user's accuracy[cite: 1].

What the numbers show

Built-up land dominates the classified extent at roughly two-thirds of the total area, consistent with Hyderabad's dense urban core, its IT and industrial corridors, and the steady peripheral development pushing outward from the city centre[cite: 1]. Green spaces — parks, agricultural land, and remaining tree cover — make up close to a quarter of the region, acting as the city's main pockets of vegetated land[cite: 1]. Waterbodies, including lakes and reservoirs such as Hussain Sagar and the wider tank network the city is historically known for, account for a modest but ecologically important share of the area, while barren or exposed land is the smallest class, largely limited to construction sites and fallow patches[cite: 1].
